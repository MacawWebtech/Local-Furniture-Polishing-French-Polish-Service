What Is French Polishing?

Understand shellac, hand application and the importance of choosing a suitable finish.

By Walnut & Grain Editorial9 October 20265 min readFinishing

A CLOSER LOOK

Understanding your furniture finish

Start with the surface, its condition and how the piece is used.

Use this guide to prepare your questions before an assessment. A clear photograph of the whole piece and a close-up of the surface help explain what you are seeing.

Discuss your furniture ↗

A technique, not a generic polish

French polishing uses a pad to apply thin layers of shellac dissolved in alcohol. It is a finishing technique rather than a generic name for every polished wooden surface.

Suitability comes first

Suitability depends on the wood, existing finish and intended use. Shellac can develop depth and controlled sheen, but needs protection from heat, standing water and alcohol.

What to discuss during assessment

An assessment should establish what can be retained, what needs preparation and the appearance to be agreed before work begins.

Before treating your own piece

Identify the existing finish and discuss appropriate care or treatment. Avoid improvised methods on an unknown surface, and seek specialist advice for valuable historic objects.
